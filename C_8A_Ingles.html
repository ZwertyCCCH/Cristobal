Romeo and Juliet is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare about two young star-crossed lovers whose deaths ultimately reconcile their feuding families. The play is set in Verona, Italy, where the Montagues and Capulets have been engaged in a bitter feud for many years. Romeo, the son of Montague, attends a masquerade ball at the Capulet house and falls in love with Juliet, the daughter of Capulet. They secretly marry with the help of Friar Laurence. Soon after, Romeo kills Juliet's cousin Tybalt in a street brawl and is banished from Verona. Juliet's parents arrange for her to marry Paris, but Friar Laurence gives her a potion that makes her appear dead. Romeo, believing Juliet is truly dead, drinks poison in her tomb. Juliet wakes, finds Romeo dead, and stabs herself. The families, devastated by the tragedy, finally end their feud. The play explores themes of love, fate, family, and the destructive nature of hatred.
